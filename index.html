5 principles of good sleep health

  1. ValueRecognize that sleep is essential and important to your health. Quality sleep is just as crucial as water or food.
  2. PrioritizePut sleep first. This is something that needs action.
  3. PersonalizeUnderstand your own sleep habits. Identify how many hours you need to feel alert and energized the following day.
  4. TrustTrials and errors. Accept that this is a journey of growth and learning about your own body. Sleep is a natural process.
  5. ProtectSleep is protected by habit and pattern. Find a pattern that works for you. Observe what environment provides the best sleep.

(Espie, 2022)

Why sleep matters

  • Over half of Americans report sleep problems.
  • Poor sleep affects motivation, mood, and thinking.
  • Poor sleep raises the risk of diabetes, heart disease, and other health problems.
  • These effects happen even when sleep problems are mild.

(Irish et al., 2015)

What sleep hygiene is

Sleep hygiene means daily habits and bedroom conditions that support good sleep.

  • It’s low-cost, easy to share, and doesn’t require a clinician.
  • People often prefer it over other sleep strategies and stick with it.
  • It works best as a first step, not as the only treatment for insomnia.

(De Pasquale et al., 2024)

Tips to best support

Keep a regular wake time

A steady wake time matters more than a fixed bedtime.

Limit caffeine near bedtime

Large amounts close to bed clearly disrupt sleep.

Avoid alcohol before bed

It helps people fall asleep but causes lighter, broken sleep later in the night.

Exercise regularly

It modestly improves sleep, and exercising at night doesn’t seem to hurt sleep.

Manage stress before bed

Relaxation and mindfulness can calm the mind and body.

Reduce bedroom noise

Earplugs or white noise can help.

Avoid nicotine

Smoking disrupts sleep, though sleep may get worse for a few weeks after quitting.

Sleep Research in Occupational Therapy

Ho and Siu (2022) tested a 4-week sleep program for adults with insomnia.

  • Used the Person-Environment-Occupation-Performance model designed by occupational therapists.
  • The program covered three areas: the person (sleep education, calming activities), the environment (bedroom changes, sleep aids), and occupation (balancing daily activities).
  • Weekly goal-setting and coaching helped people make changes in their own routines.
  • It worked better than sleep hygiene education and relaxation alone.
  • People who slept longer and better were associated with less depression and anxiety.
  • Their balance of daily activities improved, and gains kept growing after the program ended.
  • Limitations: small study, not randomized, one-month follow-up.
